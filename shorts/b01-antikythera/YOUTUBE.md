## Description
A dramatised story. The craftsman and the king are imagined; the Antikythera Mechanism is real.

In 1901, sponge divers lifted a corroded lump of bronze from a Roman shipwreck off the Greek island of Antikythera. X-rays later showed about thirty surviving bronze gears inside. The dials tracked the Moon, and one gear matched the 19-year Metonic cycle while another matched the 223-month Saros eclipse cycle. Experts still debate who built it and exactly when, with estimates from about 205 BCE to 87 BCE.
